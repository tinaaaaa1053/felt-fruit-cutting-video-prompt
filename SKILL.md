# Felt Fruit Cutting Video Prompt

Create two copy-ready versions of the same prompt: natural Chinese followed by natural English. Preserve the handcrafted needle-felted aesthetic through the fruit exterior, internal anatomy, knife, cut surfaces, fallen pieces, motion, and sound. Do not produce a generic fruit-name substitution.

## Plan the fruit internally

Infer sensible details when the user supplies only a fruit name. Ask a question only when the fruit is ambiguous or the user requires an exact cut style, setting, duration, or palette that cannot be inferred.

Decide before writing:

- the fruit's recognizable silhouette, size, skin color, and surface details;
- its defining cross-section: segments, core, seeds, pit, rind, chambers, fibers, or color gradient;
- a cut direction and slice shape that reveal that anatomy clearly;
- how many cuts can remain readable in one fixed macro composition;
- the dry, soft sounds caused by felt fibers compressing and separating.

Read [references/fruit-anatomy.md](references/fruit-anatomy.md) when handling a new fruit family, choosing an anatomically coherent cut, or repairing a failed result.

The three user-authored bilingual source prompts are preserved in [references/original-prompts.md](references/original-prompts.md). Read them only when the user asks for an original, comparison, or source-anchored revision.

## Keep every visible component felted

Describe the fruit as thumb-sized and handcrafted from needle-felted wool. Show fine loose fibers, compacted wool, subtle needle-punch marks, soft irregularities, and a tactile handmade finish.

Carry the same material logic inside the fruit:

- render flesh as lighter or contrasting layers of compacted and airy wool;
- render seeds as tiny embedded felt knots or stitched fiber details;
- render citrus membranes, rind bands, cores, or chambers as thin felt layers;
- render pits as dense felt forms and plan the cut around them;
- keep cut faces fibrous, soft, and three-dimensional.

The knife must also be miniature felted wool: soft and fluffy in appearance with a neat, straight edge. Present the cutting as a gentle stylized craft animation. Never introduce a hidden metal blade, hard plastic core, real steel glint, realistic juice, wet pulp, droplets, or edible flesh.

## Compose the bilingual prompt

Use this exact section order for each language unless the user requests another format:

### Chinese

1. `🧶 中文版本`
2. `主题：`
3. `声音风格：`
4. `视觉效果：`
5. `灯光与镜头：`
6. `动作：`

### English

1. `✨ English Version`
2. `Theme:`
3. `Sound Style:`
4. `Visual Style:`
5. `Lighting & Camera:`
6. `Action:`

Write idiomatically in both languages. Do not expose variables, planning notes, or alternatives inside the finished prompt.

## Build one observable scene

Include these elements in both versions:

- one natural hand holding the miniature felt knife;
- one miniature felt fruit fully visible on a neutral fabric surface or wooden cutting board;
- ultra-clear 4K macro close-up, fully fixed camera, and shallow depth of field;
- focus on the contact point while keeping the active slice readable;
- soft natural window light with warm highlights and gentle shadows;
- several steady, gentle cutting motions in one continuous shot;
- each completed slice separating visibly, tipping softly onto the board, and retaining its fluffy volume;
- a calm, tidy, warm, therapeutic mood.

Describe visible intermediate motion: the felt edge lightly compresses the surface, passes through the fibers, the cut face opens, and the finished piece settles. Avoid teleporting from whole fruit to pre-cut slices.

## Design dry felt ASMR

Keep sound synchronized, close, light, and material-specific:

- soft wool-on-wool friction at first contact;
- a muted fiber-compression or gentle felt-shearing sound through the cut;
- a faint plush landing as each slice touches the board;
- subtle cloth ambience or a restrained wooden-board contact only when visible.

Exclude speech, music, wet slicing, juice squelches, crisp produce snaps, metallic scraping, hard knife taps, and exaggerated impacts unless the user explicitly requests a mixed-material variant.

## Enforce continuity

Integrate concise constraints into the action rather than appending a large negative list. Always preserve:

- one continuous take with no jump cuts, time skips, speed ramps, or hidden transitions;
- stable fruit size, colors, position, knife shape, hand anatomy, lighting, and background;
- the same number of pieces before and after every cut;
- no duplicated, respawned, pre-sliced, reattached, floating, or prematurely falling pieces;
- no morphing fingers, extra hands, text, subtitles, logos, or unrelated props;
- the knife completes one cut before starting the next and does not saw rapidly or pass through a pit;
- slices fall only after the corresponding cut reaches the board.

For long or round fruit, keep the remaining fruit stable with gentle fingertip support placed safely away from the visible cut line. Do not let the supporting hand obscure the cross-section.

## Align Chinese and English

Treat the two versions as parallel deliverables. Verify that both contain the same:

- fruit variety and count;
- external and internal colors;
- anatomy and cut orientation;
- number or cadence of cuts;
- knife material and background;
- lighting, framing, focus, and continuity constraints;
- sound events and emotional tone.

Translate meaning, not sentence structure. Correct awkward source phrasing while keeping all factual details aligned.

## Final check

Before returning the result, verify that:

- the cross-section belongs to the named fruit rather than generic lighter-colored flesh;
- all visible fruit structures remain wool felt after cutting;
- the knife looks soft but separates the fibers cleanly in a clearly stylized scene;
- no wet, metallic, plastic, clay, foam, or photoreal edible material appears;
- the cuts and falling pieces form a physically traceable sequence;
- Chinese and English describe the same finished video;
- the result contains no placeholders or production commentary.

Return the two finished prompts directly. Add a short explanation only when the user asks for design reasoning or provides a failed generation that needs diagnosis.
