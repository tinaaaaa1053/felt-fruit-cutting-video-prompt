# Felt Fruit Anatomy and Cutting Logic

Use the closest fruit family, then customize for the named variety. Every anatomical detail must be made from wool felt; the guide describes visual design, not real food preparation.

## Quick routing

| Fruit family | Exterior cues | Felt cross-section | Default cut plan | Continuity risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Berries | compact round or heart shape; fine fiber seeds or clustered lobes | soft central flesh, subtle radial gradient, embedded seed knots | two to four thin crosswise or lengthwise slices | seeds turning into real seeds or loose beads |
| Banana and plantain | curved silhouette, yellow or green peel band, dark felt tip | pale cream wool center with a thin peel ring and tiny central fiber specks | steady crosswise coin slices from one end | fruit straightens, peel disappears, slices multiply |
| Citrus | dimpled rind, round form, leaf or stem only if useful | distinct radial segments, pale pith ring, thin membranes, small embedded seeds when appropriate | crosswise rounds to reveal the radial pattern, or one half cut followed by a wedge | real juice, transparent pulp, changing segment count |
| Pome fruit | apple or pear silhouette, stem, skin gradient | pale felt flesh, central star-like core, small dark felt seed knots | slice from one side toward the core; use a final half cut to reveal the core | generic interior, loose seeds, knife jumping through core |
| Stone fruit | seam line, softly blushed skin, small stem | colored flesh around one dense contrasting felt pit | make several outer slices, then reveal the intact pit; never slice through it | pit vanishes or becomes a hard real stone |
| Melon and gourd fruit | large curve scaled down to thumb size, rind bands or netting | thick rind layers, broad flesh, central fuzzy seed cavity | cut a miniature wedge into even slices with the cavity remaining coherent | excessive size, loose seed scatter, watery flesh |
| Kiwi, dragon fruit, fig | distinctive skin and high-contrast patterned center | radial color fields with tiny embedded dark felt knots | crosswise rounds with stable repeating pattern | seeds become plastic beads or pattern flickers |
| Pineapple | diamond-textured felt rind, compact felt crown | golden fibrous flesh and a slightly denser radial core | remove no crown on camera; cut the prepared fruit body into two or three rounds | crown respawns, hard rind, generic yellow interior |
| Pomegranate or passion fruit | rounded shell with crown or wrinkled skin | organized felt chambers containing clustered wool beads | one deliberate half cut, then a gentle wedge cut if space allows | real liquid, loose beads scattering, implausible many slices |
| Coconut and hard-shell fruit | layered fibrous shell rendered as dense felt | contrasting felt shell band and pale interior ring | one clean half cut in a stylized soft-felt transformation | shell turns hard, knife becomes metal, shards appear |
| Grapes and small clustered fruit | one selected fruit or a compact miniature cluster | soft translucent-looking color gradient expressed only through fibers | slice one enlarged thumb-tip-sized fruit; keep the cluster out of frame | many tiny objects duplicate or roll away |

## Surface and interior vocabulary

Prefer tactile felt language:

- fine wool fibers, airy nap, compacted wool layers, needle-punch marks;
- embroidered dots, embedded fiber knots, stitched seed details;
- soft rind band, fuzzy membrane, radial felt segments, dense felt core;
- fluffy cut face, gently compressed edge, soft three-dimensional slice.

Avoid terms that push the model toward other materials:

- juicy, dripping, wet, translucent pulp, sticky juice;
- glossy plastic, rubber, foam, clay, sponge;
- razor-sharp, steel edge, metallic reflection, hard shell fracture.

When color depth is needed, use layered wool shades rather than gloss or liquid translucency.

## Cut selection

- Use crosswise coin slices for elongated fruit and fruit with radial patterns.
- Use lengthwise slices when the silhouette or central anatomy is the main identifier.
- Use one half cut for chambered fruit whose interior would become visually unstable across many slices.
- Use outer-flesh slices followed by a pit reveal for stone fruit.
- Keep the number of visible pieces low enough to track, normally three to five.
- Ensure the fruit and every resulting piece remain inside the fixed frame.

## Color discipline

Use two to four coordinated wool shades:

- one exterior color;
- one lighter or contrasting flesh color;
- one restrained rind, pith, core, seed, or accent color;
- optional natural blush or gradient formed by blended fibers.

Keep the colors stable between the intact fruit and the cut faces. Do not introduce rainbow interiors unless requested.

## Failure-directed repairs

- Real fruit appears: repeat “entirely handcrafted from needle-felted wool” in the exterior, interior, and fallen-slice sentences; replace juicy adjectives with fiber vocabulary.
- Metal knife appears: specify a single-piece miniature felt knife with visible wool fibers, matte softness, and no internal blade or metallic reflections.
- Interior is generic: name the defining cross-section and its spatial arrangement, such as radial citrus segments or an apple's star-shaped core.
- Jump cut creates slices: describe compression, fiber separation, exposed cut face, and settling as four visible phases in real time.
- Slices duplicate or vanish: state the starting fruit count, planned cut count, and final piece count when the user needs strict continuity.
- Fruit rolls or changes size: add gentle fingertip support and keep the fruit centered on the board throughout.
- Slices fall before separation: state that each slice stays attached until the felt edge reaches the board, then tips once and comes to rest.
- English and Chinese diverge: compare fruit count, anatomy, palette, cut direction, background, sound events, and final piece behavior line by line before returning.
